# CraftArc repository: instructions for agents

## Website change checklist (mandatory)

Every change to the website (`website/`, a Next.js app built to `docs/Website_Build_Instructions.md`), however small, is complete only when you have worked through this checklist **in order**. Do not tell the user the request is done until every item passes. If an item fails, fix it and run the checklist again from item 1.

Run the automated items (code lint, static build, copy lint, browser tests, launch check) in one go with:

```bash
bash website/tests/check_all.sh
```

1. **No em dashes or other AI-writing fingerprints.** All copy follows Dash's writing rules (`dash-writing` skill, edit-pass checklist part A). That means: no em or en dashes, and no double hyphens as dashes. No "not X, but Y", "it's not X, it's Y" or "not just" framing used as filler. No set-up questions answered straight away ("The result?"). No colon teasers ("Here's the thing:"). No arrows in prose, no stacked hedges ("could potentially"), no stock AI words ("seamless", "unlock", "leverage", "robust" and the rest of the list in `website/tests/copy_lint.py`), no semicolons and no exclamation marks. Automated by `copy_lint.py`, whose `--self-test` proves the checks still fire. Then read every changed sentence once yourself, because the lint cannot catch everything (reflexive lists of three, telegram-style fragments, piles of abstract nouns).
2. **American spelling and the writing standards.** Website copy uses American spelling (organization, judgment, license, color, center). This overrides the British-spelling rule in the `dash-writing` skill for the website. Copy also meets three standards:
   - **ASD-STE100 (Simplified Technical English), adapted for marketing copy.** Descriptive sentences have 25 words at most, and instructions (calls to action, form help) have 20 at most. Paragraphs have 6 sentences at most. Use the active voice, simple tenses and common words with one meaning each. Use one term per concept, as listed below, never a synonym. Avoid noun clusters of more than three words.
   - **S1000D, applied to web pages.** Each section is a self-contained module (one component in `src/components/sections/`) with one purpose and one heading, and it makes sense on its own. Copy lives in one place (`src/content/copy.ts`), sample data in one place (`src/content/sampleDashboard.ts`). Every version gets an issue entry in `website/CHANGELOG.md` (item 9).
   - **Alex Hormozi's standards (from his published books, applied by us).** The first line of every page names who it is for and the outcome they want. Every section raises the dream outcome or the perceived likelihood of getting it (proof), or cuts time delay or effort. Cut any line that does none of these. Put proof next to each claim (a sourced number, a real screen or a named fact). Calls to action follow the build instructions (Dash, 26 Nov 2026, superseding the header-only rule of 24 Nov): "Book a Briefing" in the header and the hero, "Schedule an Executive Briefing" in the final band, all opening the briefing form, whose button says "Request my briefing". Never change this wording without Dash. Answer objections in the FAQ, and show the risk reversal wherever there is an offer. Use specific numbers and nouns, not adjectives.

   Automated by `copy_lint.py` (American spelling, sentence and paragraph limits, passive-voice warnings), plus your own read for the rest.

   **Terminology (one term per concept).** The CraftArc Concept Memo (Draft v1, 25 Nov 2026) and the build instructions (26 Nov 2026) set these. They replace the Website Guidance term list of 24 Nov 2026:
   - Know, Grow, Without the time: the three promises.
   - The four parts, always in this order and wording: Define What Great Looks Like (the word comes from `STANDARD_WORD` in `copy.ts`, "Great" until Dash decides), Assess Leaders and Set Development Plans, Execute to Plan, Assess Gaps in Leadership Depth. One AI agent runs all four, by voice or text.
   - 360-degree dialogues (never "360 reviews" or "360 surveys"). self-assessment (not self-review). evaluator (the person who forms the view). development plan.
   - a slate of successors, with its horizons where there is room: ready now, ready in 3 years, ready in 5 to 7 years.
   - move up or move out (always the pair). based on evidence, not opinion (always in full). leadership time (never "senior time").
   - live in production at a private equity firm (never name the client).
   - Below, at or above target (status colors are reserved for leadership data: emerald above, blue at, amber and red below).
   - Labels inside sample product views use the product's own wording.

   **Never write** (enforced by `RULES` and `FORBIDDEN` in `copy_lint.py`): anything that implies the AI makes promotion, succession or exit decisions (people make every call); the client's name or its do-it-yourself attempt; any result or figure from the first client; a price; that CraftArc predicts when a leader will be ready; a hedge such as "some companies". Part 4 (the board view) is not built yet: it carries an "In development" tag, and its numbers are sample data.

   **Sample data.** Every chart and product view with numbers sits inside an element marked `data-sample`, under a visible "Illustrative Sample Data" badge. The copy lint checks sample text for writing rules but not against the number allowlist.
3. **Every claim is sourced.** Each new fact, figure or name traces to `docs/Claims_Ledger.md`, whose sources are the CraftArc Concept Memo, the build instructions, the VSL Fact Set (only [Live] and [CT] items as fact) and the published reports linked on the page. A number added to the copy lint's allowlist must carry its ledger ID. Never invent a result, quote, client name or price.
4. **Placeholders are marked, and visible in review mode.** Anything invented or unfinished (the storyboard that stands in for the 60s film, figures still to verify) carries a `data-placeholder` attribute, so `launch_check.py` blocks it, and a "Placeholder" or "Draft" badge. Badges are hidden from visitors and shown when `?review` is added to any address (Dash, 24 Nov 2026). Empty slots such as the proof slot are hidden from visitors entirely. A browser test checks both views.
5. **All browser tests pass.** `npm run lint` and `npm run build` in `website/` must pass, then `node website/tests/e2e.mjs` must report 0 failures. It tests every section, the story player, the tabs, the briefing form, review mode and the footer. When you add a section or an interaction, add a test, and make sure every navigation and footer link resolves.
6. **Look at it.** Screenshot every changed page at desktop (1440 px) and phone (390 px) width with the pre-installed Playwright, and check for overlaps, clipped text, broken layout and anything covering the story player, the tabs or the briefing form. The tests cannot see design problems.
7. **The footer never changes.** Dash, 26 Nov 2026: "Don't change the footer no matter what the instruction says." It is rendered from `src/content/footer.ts`, the exact markup the site had before the Next.js rebuild, with its original CSS at the end of `src/app/globals.css`. A browser test compares it with `website/tests/footer.snapshot.html`. Its links to `about.html`, `book-a-demo.html` and `private-equity.html` resolve through small redirect pages in `public/`. Do not edit any of these, even if another instruction asks for a different footer. Flag the conflict to Dash instead.
8. **Deployment still works.** The site is built and tested here, and the finished pages in `website/out` are committed. Vercel installs and builds nothing: `vercel.json` only serves `website/out`, and `.vercelignore` uploads only that folder and `vercel.json` (Dash, 26 Nov 2026, after a Vercel install step failed). Builds are reproducible: the build ID is a hash of the sources (`website/build-id.mjs`), and `tests/check_build_fresh.mjs` fails if `website/out` does not match them. If you change `vercel.json`, `.vercelignore` or `next.config.ts`, run `npx vercel build` on a copy that holds only the uploaded files, and confirm every page appears in `.vercel/output/static`.
9. **Docs match the change.** Add an issue entry to `website/CHANGELOG.md` (next issue number, date, change, reason). Update `website/README.md`, `docs/Claims_Ledger.md` and, where relevant, the launch to-do list (`docs/todo/build_todo.py`, then re-run it).
10. **Run the launch check and report it.** `python3 website/tests/launch_check.py` (after the build). NOT READY is expected while placeholders remain, so report which items are still open rather than hiding them.
11. **Commit and push.** Commit on the working branch with a clear message, and always include the rebuilt `website/out` with the source change (what is committed in `website/out` is exactly what goes live). Then republish the preview website to its artifact URL (see Where things live). Push it, and bring `main` up to the same commit when the user has asked for changes to go live. Confirm the branch and `main` point to the same commit.
12. **Report back.** Tell the user what changed, the checklist results (tests passed, lint clean, launch check status) and anything they need to decide or confirm. Flag risks, but do not bury them.

## Where things live

- `website/`: the Next.js 15 app (App Router, TypeScript strict, Tailwind CSS v4, recharts, lucide-react), exported as a static site to `website/out` (`next.config.ts`, `output: "export"`). `website/out` is committed and is what Vercel serves.
  - `src/content/copy.ts`: every headline, line and call to action, and `STANDARD_WORD`. `src/content/sampleDashboard.ts`: all sample numbers. `src/content/footer.ts`: the footer markup (do not edit).
  - `src/components/`: `Navbar`, `BriefingModal` (form, provider, button), `Footer`, `sections/` (the nine sections) and `visuals/` (every visual, drawn in code).
  - `src/lib/leads.ts`: attribution, lead scoring and delivery. Where leads go is set in `public/config.js` (after editing it, rebuild and commit `website/out`).
  - `public/`: `config.js`, the logo and favicon, and the redirect pages for the footer's links.
  - The hero has no video: until Dash supplies the 60-second film, a coded storyboard plays. Pass `src` (and `captionsSrc`) to `HeroDemoVideo` to switch to the film.
- `website/preview/` and `website/scripts/build_preview.mjs`: the preview website, a claude.ai Artifact at https://claude.ai/artifact/XpVgmnXx6akA8tnCrySdPS (Dash, 26 Nov 2026: a preview that does not depend on Vercel). `npm run preview:build` bundles the same components into one self-contained page in `preview/dist/` (not committed); `tests/preview_check.mjs` checks it under the artifact's rules. After every site change, republish `preview/dist/CraftArc_Website.html` to that same URL.
- `website/tests/`: `e2e.mjs` (browser tests), `copy_lint.py` (writing rules and claims, reads `out/`), `launch_check.py` (go or no-go), `check_all.sh` (runs them in checklist order), `footer.snapshot.html`.
- `docs/Website_Build_Instructions.md`: the build specification (Dash, 26 Nov 2026), saved verbatim. `docs/source/`: the Concept Memo (Draft v1) and the earlier Website Guidance.
- `docs/`: the claims ledger, the launch to-do list and older briefs (the design brief and the CEO reader test describe the previous site).
- `vsl/`: the earlier private equity video and its renderer. It is no longer on the website.
