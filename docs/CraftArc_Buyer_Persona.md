## Buyer 4: PE COO or managing partner who wants to build the next set of leaders

**The person.**
- COO, chief of staff or managing partner at a mid-tier private equity firm of roughly $1B to $10B AUM, on its second or third fund, often past $2B, where the fund has to develop itself as an institution (Fact Set §1, a working view).
- Has said, inside the firm or in public, that building the next generation of partners is part of the job.
- The firm runs a principal, MD and partner structure, with an annual review cycle that closes between January and March for most firms (working view).
- Pays for: an HR or performance tool that sits on the shelf, external coaches or assessment centres for a few people, and the senior hours that go into reviews.
- Standing objection: "We tried to do this with AI ourselves. Why would a product hold our framework when Claude could not?"

**What drives them.**
- Values evidence over opinion, partner time, the firm's culture and a fair, defensible partner-readiness call.
- Distrusts HR software that adds admin, tools that nag people, AI that makes the judgement, and anything that disrupts the cycle already in place.
- Buys as the business sponsor. HR acts, the business asks (Candida). Security and data handling go to the COO or head of IT.
- Budget: a flat annual fee in the tens of thousands is an easy decision. A per-seat negotiation is not.
- Reads fast and top-down. Wants the answer, then the proof, then the risk.

**The test.** Would they book a demo and bring their framework to it?

### Context

**Words to mirror:** what great looks like, our framework, competencies, principal, MD, partner, partner-ready, evaluation coordinator, Executive Committee, contributors, evidence, the next deal, the review cycle, the senior bench, culture.

**Angles that resonate:**
- Their own framework, run as a working process, without code.
- Evidence graded by source, and gaps flagged before the cycle closes.
- A writeup that admits what the evidence cannot answer.
- Respect for partners' time: one conversation, no nagging.
- Proof from a peer firm that tried the DIY AI route and then bought.
- A low-risk start: the senior team first, an implementation consultant, and an exit right.

**Avoid:** HR jargon such as "talent management suite"; "performance management"; "AI decides"; scores for individuals; unsourced ROI; guarantees of participation or outcomes; naming the anchor client; anything that sounds like a long implementation.

**What they already have or could use instead:** the annual review in spreadsheets and email; generic 360 survey tools; external executive coaches; a general AI assistant with a long prompt, as the anchor client tried.

### Meta question

**"Will this give us a fair, evidence-based view of who is ready for the next level, in time to act on it, without adding work for our partners?"**

**Feeder questions**
1. Does it run on our framework, our roles and our steps?
2. Will our partners and contributors actually take part?
3. Can I trust the evidence behind each rating?
4. Who makes the judgement, the tool or us?
5. How much partner time does it take?
6. How fast can we start, and how much disruption will it cause?
7. Who else like us uses it?
8. What happens if it does not work?

### Leader test (alongside the 1 to 10 score)

Mark each check pass or fail.
1. **Answer.** Does the opening tell them, in their words, what they get?
2. **Fit.** Is it clear it runs on their own framework?
3. **Trust.** Is every claim about the product shown on a real screen or sourced?
4. **Judgement.** Is it clear the firm, not the AI, makes the call?
5. **Effort.** Is the partner time and the disruption clear and low?
6. **Proof.** Is there a peer they recognise as like them?
7. **Next step.** Is the ask clear, small and worth their time?
